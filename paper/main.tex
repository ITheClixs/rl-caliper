  \ruledtitle{Where Run-to-Run Spread in RL Post-Training Comes From,\\
  and When One Run Can Predict It}
  \vspace{6pt}
  {\centering
    \normalsize Mehmet Demir G\"uven$^{\dagger}$\par\vspace{2pt}
    \normalsize\textbf{ETH Z\"urich}$^{*}$\par\vspace{1pt}
    \small\texttt{dgueven@student.ethz.ch}\par
  }
  \vspace{6pt}
  \begin{minipage}{\textwidth}
  \begin{center}\textbf{Abstract}\end{center}
  \vspace{-4pt}
  \begin{quote}
  Reinforcement learning post-training is reported from single runs, and how much a rerun would
  move the number is established only by repeating the training. We ask what one run can be made
  to say about it.

  Training noise is not accumulated. Replacing every reward with a coin flip, which leaves the
  noise and the step sizes untouched, restores accumulation and grows divergence by a factor of
  $\numNullCoin$. A perturbation entering at one update is reweighted by every update that
  follows, so the covariance of a run about its mean obeys
  $S_{t+1} = A_t S_t A_t^{\!\top} + Q_t$. Carrying the gradient of the reported metric backwards
  along one stored trajectory turns that recursion into the across-seed variance of the metric, as
  a sum of scalars with no covariance matrix formed. The transpose the backward pass needs is
  free, because the mean update of a count-based estimator is the gradient of a scalar potential.

  Tested prospectively over $\numForecastSettings$ settings, one run trained and frozen before the
  rest existed, the forecast has a median error of $\numForecastMedian\times$. On a pretrained
  model it is short by a factor of twenty-five, because the runs end in groups rather than
  scattered around one. What survives is the localisation: the per-update kernel puts
  $\numRealFirstHalfShare\%$ of the variance in the first half of the run, and the run stops injecting
  any after the fifth update.
    \end{quote}
  \end{minipage}
  \vspace{6pt}
  \begin{minipage}{\textwidth}
    \centering
    {\includegraphics[width=0.96\textwidth]{figures/teaser.pdf}}
    \captionof{figure}{\textbf{Left:} the across-seed standard deviation of the reported pass
    rate, forecast from a single frozen run, against the value realised over $\numForecastSeeds$
    independent runs
    trained afterwards. Bars are bootstrap intervals over runs; the solid line is equality and the
    dotted lines a factor of $1.5$. \textbf{Right:} predicted against measured policy divergence
    for the models this one replaces. Propagating the covariance tracks it everywhere. Treating
    noise as accumulating tracks it too where the prompts in a pool are unlike each other, and
    fails by a factor of two where they are alike, which is where the objective supplies the most
    curvature. Compressing the filter to a single timescale fails by two orders of magnitude
    throughout.}

  Measuring, Not Tuning, the Batch and Step Size of RL Post-Training}
  \vspace{6pt}
  {\centering
    \normalsize Mehmet Demir G\"uven$^{\dagger}$\par\vspace{2pt}
    \normalsize\textbf{ETH Z\"urich}\par\vspace{1pt}
    \small\texttt{dgueven@student.ethz.ch}\par

  \vspace{6pt}
  \begin{minipage}{\textwidth}
  \begin{center}\textbf{Abstract}\end{center}
  \vspace{-4pt}
  \begin{quote}
  Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards samples twice: once over prompts, once over
responses within each prompt. How a fixed rollout budget should be divided between the two is
currently a tuned constant, reported without justification and re-tuned whenever anything changes.
We show it is a measurement. For binary rewards we derive the exact finite-$G$ mean and covariance
of the advantage estimators in current use, which yields two results. First, the estimators differ
only through a weight $\lambda(p,G)$ on prompt difficulty; when $\lambda$ does not depend on the
pass rate, as for RLOO and mean-baseline GRPO, they are the same estimator and the step size
absorbs the difference exactly. Second, the gradient noise splits into a between-prompt term
$\tau_b$ that more responses cannot reduce and a within-prompt term $\tau_w$ that falls as
$1/(G-1)$. Optimising the split then gives a closed form, $G^{\star} = 1 + \sqrt{\tau_w/\tau_b}$,
and both traces are estimable from the microbatch gradients a trainer already accumulates, at no
extra rollout cost. Measured this way, across enumerable policies, transformers trained from
scratch and a pretrained model, $G^{\star}$ lands between three and six. Common recipes use eight
to sixty-four. Pricing prefill sharing into the objective accounts for the gap rather than
contradicting it: the optimum then solves a one-line stationarity condition that is increasing in
both the prefill ratio and the batch size, and at the ratios we measure on real hardware it ranges
from three to fifty-one. Which end of that range a run belongs at is fixed by its
prompt-to-response length ratio, so a group size of sixteen is a defensible reading of one regime
and a poor one for another. Either way it is a measurement, not a constant.
  \end{quote}

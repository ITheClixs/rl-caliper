  \ruledtitle{How Many Runs Does an RL Post-Training Comparison Need?\\
  Forecasting Run-to-Run Spread from a Single Run}
  \vspace{6pt}
  {\centering
    \normalsize Mehmet Demir G\"uven$^{\dagger}$\par\vspace{2pt}
    \normalsize\textbf{ETH Z\"urich}$^{*}$\par\vspace{1pt}
    \small\texttt{dgueven@student.ethz.ch}\par
  }
  \vspace{6pt}
  \begin{minipage}{\textwidth}
  \begin{center}\textbf{Abstract}\end{center}
  \vspace{-4pt}
  \begin{quote}
  Reinforcement learning post-training is compared from single runs, and how many runs a
  comparison needs is set by a quantity nobody measures: how much a rerun would move the score.
  The usual recourse is to fix the run count in advance, or to spend runs estimating the spread
  before the comparison can begin, because after one run per arm there is no empirical spread to
  put in a stopping rule.

  We forecast it instead. Training noise is not accumulated: a perturbation entering at one update
  is reweighted by every update that follows, so the covariance of a run about its mean obeys
  $S_{t+1} = A_t S_t A_t^{\!\top} + Q_t$. Carrying the gradient of the reported metric backwards
  along one stored trajectory turns that recursion into the across-seed variance of the metric, as
  a sum of scalars with no covariance matrix formed. On enumerable policies, where every input is
  exact, and frozen before the remaining seeds existed, it has a median error of
  $\numForecastMedian\times$ over $\numForecastSettings$ settings of at most $25$ updates.

  That is a statement about runs that barely move: those settings gain at most
  $\numHeadlineGainMax$ in pass rate. Raised until runs learn, the same tier breaks the forecast
  with every input exact, by more than $3\times$ in $\numRegradeCarriedOverThree\%$ of
  $\numRegradeLearningCount$ settings and by orders of magnitude once a policy collapses.
  Linearising the model instead of the run survives it there: seeds simulated in the tangent model
  at the starting policy, with sampling and saturation kept exact and no run trained, are within
  $1.5\times$ in $\numRegradeTangentWithin\%$ of those settings, about what ten real seeds achieve.

  Used to size comparisons whose ordering is clear, with every stopping threshold chosen on other
  tasks before it is applied, it reaches a $\numSizeTarget\%$ mistake rate in
  $\numHeldStrictAdjointRuns$ runs, $\numSplitFixedGainLow$ to $\numSplitFixedGainHigh\times$ fewer
  than a fixed budget measured below its cap and $\numHeldStrictEmpiricalGain\times$ fewer than
  estimating the deviation from spent seeds. A closed form that needs no run comes within
  $\numHeldStrictFrozenGain\times$ once its threshold is learned on other tasks; the stored run's
  scale replaces that learning. Near-ties are settled by no policy within the seeds we have. Corrupting the forecast on purpose puts the point where it stops paying at
  about $\numTolLooseBreakeven\times$ relative error.

  On pretrained models accuracy is regime-dependent. Across $\numSweepBands$ pre-specified regimes
  on Qwen2.5-0.5B the frozen forecast misses by factors of $\numSweepErrors$, one of them inside a
  criterion declared before the sweep ran and all of them inside that break-even. A corpus whose
  reward groups go unanimous is missed by $\numRealShortCheap\times$ although its measured seed spread is the
  largest we report, and most of its runs turn out to answer one constant whatever they are
  asked; Qwen2.5-7B under Adam on the same task is short by $\numAdamScaleShortfallLow$ to
  $\numAdamScaleShortfallHigh\times$. We report where it fails as well as where it works.
      \end{quote}
  \end{minipage}
  \vspace{6pt}
  \begin{minipage}{\textwidth}
    \centering
    {\includegraphics[width=0.96\textwidth]{figures/teaser.pdf}}
    \captionof{figure}{\textbf{Left:} the across-seed standard deviation of the reported pass
    rate, forecast from a single frozen run, against the value realised over $\numForecastSeeds$
    independent runs
    trained afterwards. Bars are bootstrap intervals over runs; the solid line is equality and the
    dotted lines a factor of $1.5$. \textbf{Right:} predicted against measured policy divergence
    for the models this one replaces. Propagating the covariance tracks it everywhere. Treating
    noise as accumulating tracks it too where the prompts in a pool are unlike each other, and
    fails by a factor of two where they are alike, which is where the objective supplies the most
    curvature. Compressing the filter to a single timescale fails by two orders of magnitude
    throughout.}

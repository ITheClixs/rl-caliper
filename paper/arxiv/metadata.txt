Title:
  Where Run-to-Run Spread in RL Post-Training Comes From, and When One Run
  Can Predict It

Authors:
  Mehmet Demir Guven (ETH Zurich)

Abstract (plain text, as pasted into the submission form):
  Reinforcement learning post-training is reported from single runs, and how much a rerun
  would move the number is established the only way it currently can be: by repeating the
  training. We show that a run already contains enough information to forecast its own
  spread.

  The correction that makes this possible is to stop treating training noise as
  accumulating. A perturbation entering at one update is passed through every later update
  and reweighted by them, so the covariance of a run about its mean obeys
  S_{t+1} = A_t S_t A_t^T + Q_t. Measuring the spectrum of A_t shows this is a filter and
  not a contraction: only a third to a half of directions contract, the leading direction
  expands, and the contracting timescales span tens of updates to effectively unbounded.
  Carrying the gradient of the reported metric backwards along one stored trajectory then
  gives the across-seed variance of that metric as a sum of scalars, one per update, with no
  covariance matrix formed. Ablating that backward pass shows it can usually be skipped:
  holding the adjoint fixed changes the forecast by a median of 2%, leaving an estimator
  that costs one extra pass per update.

  Tested prospectively -- one run trained and frozen, the prediction recorded, and only then
  the remaining seeds trained -- the forecast has a median absolute error of 1.11x across 192
  settings. Carried to a pretrained model it underpredicts by a factor of twelve, because
  eight runs end in two distinct groups rather than scattered around one; the mechanism
  survives even so, since a run's first three batches correlate with its final score at 0.88. The filtering is caused by learning: replacing every reward with a coin flip
  leaves rollouts, batches and step sizes untouched, and divergence then grows 90x over
  eighty updates instead of ending at 0.14x of where it started.

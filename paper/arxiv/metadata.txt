Title:
  Where Run-to-Run Spread in RL Post-Training Comes From, and When One Run
  Can Predict It

Authors:
  Mehmet Demir Guven (ETH Zurich)

Abstract (plain text, as pasted into the submission form):
  Reinforcement learning post-training is reported from single runs, and how much a rerun
  would move the number is established only by repeating the training. We ask what one run
  can be made to say about it.

  Training noise is not accumulated. Replacing every reward with a coin flip, which leaves
  the noise and the step sizes untouched, restores accumulation and grows divergence by a
  factor of 90.5. A perturbation entering at one update is reweighted by every update that
  follows, so the covariance of a run about its mean obeys
  S_{t+1} = A_t S_t A_t^T + Q_t. Carrying the gradient of the reported metric backwards along
  one stored trajectory turns that recursion into the across-seed variance of the metric, as a
  sum of scalars with no covariance matrix formed. The transpose the backward pass needs is
  free, because the mean update of a count-based estimator is the gradient of a scalar
  potential.

  Tested prospectively over 192 settings, one run trained and frozen before the rest existed,
  the forecast has a median error of 1.11x. On a pretrained model it is short by a factor of
  twenty-five, because the runs end in groups rather than scattered around one. What survives
  is the localisation: the per-update kernel puts 90% of the variance in the first half of the
  run, and the run stops injecting any after the fifth update.

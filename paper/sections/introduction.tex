\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards has become the dominant way to improve reasoning in
language models \citep{deepseekr1, shao2024deepseekmath}, and a practitioner starting a run must
choose, among other things, a learning rate, a clip range, a KL coefficient, how many prompts to
put in a batch, and how many responses to sample per prompt. These choices interact, they are
re-tuned at every model scale and on every new task, and the resulting recipes are reported as
constants rather than as consequences of anything measurable.

Pretraining is not like this. The gradient noise scale predicts the batch size beyond which
parallelism stops buying speed \citep{mccandlish2018empirical, shallue2019measuring}, and $\mu$P
prescribes how a learning rate found at small width should be carried to large width
\citep{yang2021tensor, yang2022mutransfer}. Both replace a sweep with a measurement. RLVR has no
equivalent. It also has a structural feature pretraining lacks: sampling happens at
\emph{two} levels, prompts and responses, and the split of a fixed rollout budget between them is
a free parameter that no existing analysis determines.

This paper works out what determines that split, and what determines the step size, from quantities
a trainer can measure while it runs.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{The estimator zoo is one estimator and a difficulty weight.} For binary verifiable
  rewards, every advantage rule whose value depends on a response's own reward and its group's
  success count -- RLOO, GRPO with a mean baseline, standardised GRPO, and their relatives --
  computes the true per-prompt gradient scaled by a weight $\lambda(p, G)$
  (Proposition~\ref{prop:mean}). RLOO has $\lambda \equiv 1$; the mean baseline has
  $\lambda = (G-1)/G$, a constant the step size absorbs exactly. Only a $\lambda$ that depends on
  the pass rate changes what a run optimises, which is what standardisation does.

  \item \textbf{An exact finite-$G$ covariance}, and from it a hierarchical noise decomposition
  into a between-prompt term $\tau_b$ that no amount of sampling per prompt can reduce and a
  within-prompt term $\tau_w$ that shrinks as $1/(G-1)$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:cov},
  Corollary~\ref{cor:bernoulli}). For RLOO the within-prompt term is, up to one slowly varying
  scalar, the mean Bernoulli variance $\E_x[p(1-p)]$ of the pass-rate distribution -- a number
  already sitting in every training log.

  \item \textbf{A drift model in place of a curvature model.} The obvious second-order treatment of
  the objective fails badly for RLVR, understating the useful step size by more than fourfold
  against exactly computed improvement. Modelling how far the \emph{policy} moves instead is
  accurate to $3\%$ over three orders of magnitude in step size, and yields an efficiency
  $\rho = 1/(1 + \Bcrit/P)$ that a run can read off the KL it already logs: progress at a fixed
  drift budget scales as $\sqrt{\rho}$.

  \item \textbf{A closed form for the group size}, $G^{*} = 1 + \sqrt{\tau_w/\tau_b}$, independent
  of the rollouts per step, and inflated by $\sqrt{1 + c_{\mathrm{pre}}/c_{\mathrm{dec}}}$ once
  prefill sharing within a group is priced in (Proposition~\ref{prop:gstar}).

  \item \textbf{An estimator that costs no extra rollouts}, reading both noise terms off a
  $K \times 2$ grid of the microbatch gradients a trainer already accumulates, together with three
  biases that have to be handled for it to work: the badly conditioned subtractive form, prompts
  repeated within a batch, and the finite-corpus correlation induced by sampling without
  replacement.

  \item \textbf{Validation at three levels}: exactly enumerable policies where every quantity is
  computed rather than estimated; transformers trained from scratch, where a population of
  independent runs is executed as a single batched kernel; and the batch-size transfer question,
  where the drift target that maximises progress is unchanged across an eightfold change in batch
  size while the learning rate that realises it is not.
\end{itemize}

Everything in this paper runs on one laptop. That is not incidental: the claim under test is that
small-scale measurement determines large-scale choices, so a study that needed a cluster to make
the measurement would be arguing against itself.

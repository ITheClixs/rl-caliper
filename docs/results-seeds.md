# Seed divergence: results

Regenerated by `analysis/figures.py` and `analysis/tables.py`. Experiments:
`experiments/s1_contraction`, `experiments/s5_contraction_time`, `experiments/s3_real_seeds`,
`experiments/s8_null`, `experiments/s6_scale`, `experiments/p6_spectrum`.

For the forecast this underpins, see `forecast.md`. For the theory, `theory-seeds.md`.

## The phenomenon

Population members are given identical parameters and then differ only in the randomness of their
rollouts. Across 13 settings (batch 4-64, drift target 1e-4 to 1e-3), 9 stay in the regime where the
run is still learning. In those 9:

| quantity | value |
|---|---|
| tail slope of divergence against updates | -1.15 to +0.26, median -0.43 |
| what a random walk requires | +1 |

Divergence rises over the first few tens of updates and then stops. It does not accumulate.

## It is caused by the learning signal

`experiments/s8_null` runs the same configuration twice from the same warmed policy, with the same
prompts, group size and *fixed* step size. Only the reward differs: verifier, or a fair coin.

| condition | divergence at 1 | at 80 | growth | pass rate 1 -> 80 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| verifier | 1.92e-3 | 2.66e-4 | **0.14x** | 0.313 -> 0.905 |
| coin | 1.97e-3 | 1.78e-1 | **90.5x** | 0.313 -> 0.258 |

A gap of 671x at the end of the run. The control that matters: mean realised drift per update is
6.10e-3 in the learning condition against 5.17e-3 in the null, so the runs that end up closer
together are the ones that moved *further* per update.

## What the filter actually is

`experiments/p6_spectrum` takes the eigenvalues of `A_t = I + eta J_t` along a 600-update
trajectory, at three pool difficulties.

* Spectral radius **exceeds one everywhere**: `10^3(rho - 1)` runs from about 20 early to 0.1 late.
* Only **31%-51%** of directions contract, a share that grows as the run proceeds.
* Among the contracting directions, eigenvalues run from about 0.98 to 1.0000, giving decay timescales
  from roughly fifty updates to effectively unbounded.

So it is a filter, not a contraction. Divergence stays bounded over the horizons anyone trains
because expansion is slow relative to run length (1.02^25 is about 1.6), and a run long enough would
leave the regime.

## The scaling

Compared at matched pass rate rather than matched step count:

    exponent in P     -0.61, 95% CI [-1.42, +0.24]     predicted -1/2
    exponent in drift +0.59, 95% CI [+0.27, +1.04]     predicted +1/2

Both intervals contain the predicted values; the `P` interval also contains zero. Nine settings are
too few to pin a two-dimensional law, which is why the intervals rather than the point estimates are
what get reported.

**Withdrawn.** An earlier version of this note claimed `KL_inf ∝ P^-0.45 D^0.35` with R^2 = 0.94 and
that outcome spread tracks `sqrt(KL)`. The scaling was fitted on too few settings to support point
estimates, and the `sqrt(KL)` relation does not hold: fitted exponent +0.10, 95% CI [-0.03, +0.45]
against a predicted +0.5. Both claims are gone. The route that works is to propagate against the
gradient of the target metric directly (`forecast.md`).

**Superseded.** The single-timescale law `KL_inf = tau_c * D_noise` fails by a median factor of 195
against exact Monte Carlo. `experiments/s5_contraction_time` is kept as the comparator that
established this, and the spectrum measurement above says why it had to fail.

**Measurement note.** The raw spread across members is dominated by the binomial error of the
evaluation itself. At 256 samples and p ~ 0.5 that error is 0.031 while the spread being measured is
about 0.015, so the raw number is mostly noise. Everything above uses a spread corrected by
subtracting `p(1-p)/n` in variance, and 4096-8192 evaluation samples.

## Where it breaks

Four of the 13 settings leave the regime, all the same way. Under a fixed drift target the
controller raises the step size as the signal decays, and `E[p(1-p)] -> 0` as a task is solved takes
the signal with it. In the settings that pass a pass rate of about 0.94 the realised drift runs to
100x its target, divergence jumps two orders of magnitude, and the outcome spread reaches 0.24
against 0.01 elsewhere. Any drift-targeted schedule needs a floor on the signal or a cap on the
step.

## Pretrained models

**Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct**, LoRA adapters (which start at zero, so the runs begin identical), six
runs, 40 updates, P=16, G=8, on exactly verifiable short-answer tasks. Every checkpoint scored on one
fixed batch of sequences.

| updates | 1 | 2 | 5 | 10 | 20 | 30 | 40 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| divergence | 2.05e-2 | 3.56e-2 | 9.77e-2 | 1.98e-1 | 7.00e-2 | 6.33e-2 | 8.31e-2 |

Tail slope +0.14 against the +1 accumulation requires. Intervals are bootstrapped over runs rather
than over the 15 pairs, which roughly doubles the standard error; once that is done the intervals at
10 and 40 updates overlap heavily, so the peak-and-fall shape is *not* resolved at six seeds. What is
resolved is that divergence does not accumulate.

**Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct (4-bit)**, on a task the base model already mostly passes: twenty updates take
the held-out pass rate from 0.41 to 0.875 and collapse the response distribution. All 32 held-out
per-prompt rates become exactly 0 or 1, two seeds report the same number to the last decimal and
agree on every prompt, while their policies differ by 1.8e-5 to 7.7e-2 in KL. A zero measured spread
on a saturated benchmark says the metric stopped resolving the runs, not that the runs agree.

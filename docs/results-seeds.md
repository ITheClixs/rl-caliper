# Seed reconvergence: results

Regenerated by `analysis/figures.py`. Experiments: `experiments/s1_contraction`,
`experiments/s5_contraction_time`, `experiments/s3_real_seeds`.

## The phenomenon

Population members are given identical parameters and then differ only in the randomness of their
rollouts. Across 13 settings (batch 4-64, drift target 1e-4 to 1e-3), 9 stay in the regime where the
run is still learning. In those 9:

| quantity | value |
|---|---|
| tail slope of divergence against updates | -1.15 to +0.26, median -0.43 |
| what a random walk requires | +1 |
| outcome spread over the run | flat, not growing |

Divergence rises over the first few tens of updates and then stops. It does not accumulate.

## The scaling

Compared at matched pass rate rather than matched step count, which matters because settings reach a
given quality at different times:

    KL_inf  ∝  P^-0.45 · D^0.35        measured, R^2 = 0.94, n = 7
    KL_inf  ∝  P^-0.5  · D^0.5         predicted

The outcome spread tracks the divergence: between adjacent batch settings the spread ratio matches
`sqrt(KL)` to within a few percent (P=8 to P=32: KL ratio 1.55, spread ratio 1.22, `sqrt(1.55)=1.24`).

**Measurement note.** The raw spread across members is dominated by the binomial error of the
evaluation itself. At 256 samples and p ~ 0.5 that error is 0.031 while the spread being measured is
about 0.015, so the raw number is mostly noise. Everything above uses a spread corrected by
subtracting `p(1-p)/n` in variance, and 4096-8192 evaluation samples.

## What we could not get

The law factors as `KL_inf = tau_c * D_noise`. `D_noise` is measurable. `tau_c` is not, by the
obvious route: fitting a single exponential to the approach gives timescales of 0.7-7 updates with
no pattern, and the resulting predictions miss the measured levels by factors of 0.55 to 6.6
(`experiments/s5_contraction_time`, slope 0.33 against a predicted 1.0, R^2 = 0.32). The likely
cause is that the contraction has a spectrum rather than one rate: fast directions equilibrate
immediately and slow ones set the final level, so a single-exponential fit is pinned by the early
rise. The consequence is that the scaling transfers from one calibration while the absolute level
still needs that calibration.

## Where it breaks

Four of the 13 settings leave the regime, all the same way. Under a fixed drift target the
controller raises the step size as the signal decays, and `E[p(1-p)] -> 0` as a task is solved takes
the signal with it. In the settings that pass a pass rate of about 0.94 the realised drift runs to
100x its target, divergence jumps two orders of magnitude, and the outcome spread reaches 0.24
against 0.01 elsewhere. This is a failure of drift-targeted control at saturation, not of the law,
and any drift-targeted schedule needs a floor on the signal or a cap on the step.

## A pretrained model

Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct, LoRA adapters (which start at zero, so the runs begin identical), six runs,
40 updates, P=16, G=8, on exactly verifiable short-answer tasks. Every checkpoint scored on one
fixed batch of sequences.

| updates | 1 | 2 | 5 | 10 | 20 | 30 | 40 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| divergence | 2.05e-2 | 3.56e-2 | 9.77e-2 | 1.98e-1 | 7.00e-2 | 6.33e-2 | 8.31e-2 |

Peak at 10 updates, then a fall of 2.4x, then flat. Tail slope +0.22. The six runs finish at
0.835 +/- 0.044 in pass rate. Six seeds and forty updates do not establish the quantitative law on
this model; they do establish that the divergence stops growing, which is what the paper rests on.

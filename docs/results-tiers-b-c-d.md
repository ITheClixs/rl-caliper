# Results: transformers, a pretrained model, and the hardware

All numbers regenerated by `analysis/tables.py` and `analysis/figures.py` from stored runs.

## Transformers trained from scratch

A population of 32 independent transformers is warmed up on a verifiable running-sum task to a
common pass rate, the noise terms are measured at that frozen policy, and every member is then
trained from the same starting point at each split of a fixed budget of 256 rollouts per step.

| corpus `N` | `tau_b` | `tau_w` | predicted `G*` | best measured `G` |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 128 | 32.1 | 137.1 | 3.38 | 2 |
| 192 | 35.4 | 140.3 | 3.17 | 2 |
| 512 | 37.6 | 146.8 | 3.04 | 2 |
| 4096 | 37.6 | 141.8 | 2.95 | 4 |
| unbounded | 37.3 | 140.0 | 2.94 | 4 |

The predicted efficiency curve peaks at `G = 4` in every case and declines monotonically beyond it;
the measured peak sits at `G = 2` or `G = 4`, with those two within one standard error of each
other in four of the five corpora. Every corpus shows the predicted decline for `G >= 8`.

The corpus-size knob works through the finite-population factor `1 - (P-1)/(N-1)` rather than
through the task, but its effect on `G*` at the reference split is modest here because the task
itself keeps `tau_w/tau_b` in a narrow band. Temperature, chain length and modulus were also tried
as knobs; all left `G*` between 2.8 and 3.4. That stability is itself a finding: for this task
family the optimal group size is small and insensitive.

## What transfers when the batch size changes

Width was tried first and does not discriminate: over widths 32 to 128 both the learning rate and
the drift target transferred, because nothing in that sweep forced them apart. Batch size does
discriminate, and it is what the drift model actually makes a claim about.

| prompts per step `P` | 8 | 16 | 32 | 64 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| best step size | 0.0417 | 0.0786 | 0.1228 | 0.1615 |
| best drift target | 5.06e-3 | 5.29e-3 | 5.15e-3 | 3.98e-3 |
| cost of carrying the `P=8` step size | 0.0% | -9.9% | -17.9% | -22.0% |
| cost of carrying the `P=8` drift target | 0.0% | 0.0% | 0.0% | 0.0% |

The step size that is optimal at `P=8` gives up 22% of the achievable gain by `P=64`. The drift
target that is optimal at `P=8` remains the best grid point at every batch size tested.

The single-step drift coefficient `Gcal + N/P`, measured by taking a step of known size and reading
the KL, predicts a 1.7x rise in the optimal step size over this range; the measured rise is 3.9x.
The coefficient moves further during a run than it does at the initial policy, which a controller
follows and a fixed learning rate cannot.

## A pretrained model

Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct with LoRA adapters, short exact-match tasks, 40 batches per corpus.

| corpus | pass rate | `E[p(1-p)]` | `tau_b` | `tau_w` | `G*` |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| count letter | 0.280 | 0.178 | 4.35e2 | 1.16e4 | 6.15 |
| nth word | 0.326 | 0.138 | 4.88e3 | 2.01e4 | 3.03 |
| add two | 0.546 | 0.062 | 1.29e4 | 3.55e4 | 2.66 |
| mixed | 0.193 | 0.071 | 4.31e3 | 1.20e4 | 2.67 |

Across families the ratio `tau_w / E[p(1-p)]` varies by a factor of 8.8, so Corollary 3 does not
hold across families. That is the corollary's assumption failing rather than the corollary: the
constant it predicts is the score scale, and a family whose answer is one number does not share a
score scale with one whose answer is four.

Within a single family it does hold. Prompts from `count_letter` were screened with 24 samples
each and split into difficulty terciles, then re-measured:

| bucket | screened `p` | measured `p` | `E[p(1-p)]` | `tau_w` | ratio, relative |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| hardest | 0.160 | 0.253 | 0.163 | 1.15e4 | 0.90 |
| middle | 0.269 | 0.272 | 0.175 | 1.33e4 | 0.96 |
| easiest | 0.380 | 0.304 | 0.185 | 1.66e4 | 1.14 |

The ratio is constant to within 14%, against a factor of 8.8 across families. The gap between the
screened and re-measured pass rates in the outer buckets is regression to the mean, which is why
the screening sample is larger than the measurement sample and why the reported pass rate is the
re-measured one.

## The hardware

Prefill and decode timings for a 0.5B model in bfloat16 on an Apple M4, converted into the
inflation `sqrt(1 + c_pre/c_dec)` that equation (16) applies to `G*`:

| prompt tokens | response tokens | `c_pre/c_dec` | inflation |
|---|---|---|---|
| 64 | 1024 | 0.04 | 1.02 |
| 256 | 1024 | 0.16 | 1.08 |
| 1024 | 1024 | 0.68 | 1.30 |
| 64 | 64 | 0.70 | 1.31 |
| 256 | 64 | 2.63 | 1.90 |
| 1024 | 64 | 10.92 | 3.45 |

(at a group size of 8; the full grid is in the stored run.)

This is the resolution of the gap between measured optima near three to six and the eight to
sixty-four in common use. Short answers behind long instructions justify groups several times
larger than the statistical optimum. Reasoning-length responses do not.

## Carrying a whole recipe to a setting it was not tuned on

Two recipes were fixed on a tuning setting (256 rollouts per step, corpus 512) and carried unchanged
to a held-out setting (1024 rollouts per step, corpus 4096, different seed):

* **default** — group size 16, and the learning rate that was best on the tuning setting
* **measured** — the group size the probe reports on the held-out setting (`G* = 3.1`, so 4), and
  the drift target that was best on the tuning setting

The measured recipe **lost**, by 28.9% of the default's gain, paired `t = -3.01` over 32 runs.

Since the recipes differ in two ways at once, the two factors were crossed at the held-out setting.
Each group size was swept over a learning-rate grid and over a drift-target grid:

| best achievable gain | `G = 4` | `G = 16` |
|---|---|---|
| tuning a learning rate | +0.4947 (grid edge) | +0.4780 (grid edge) |
| tuning a drift target | +0.4431 | +0.3989 |
| predicted efficiency | 0.815 | 0.674 |

1. **The group-size prediction held.** `G = 4` beat `G = 16` under both parameterisations. Under
   drift control, where the step size is not a confound, by 11.1% against a predicted 10%.
2. **The drift target transfers across group size, exactly.** Both group sizes have their optimum
   at the same drift target, `1.45e-3`.
3. **It did not transfer across this change of budget.** The tuning setting's optimum was
   `3.19e-3`, a factor of 2.2 away, and using it cost 20% of the achievable gain. Earlier the
   target held to within 5% across a fourfold change in batch size and 21% across an eightfold one;
   a sixteenfold change in prompts per step combined with a fourfold change in rollout budget is
   past where it holds.
4. **A constant drift target is not the best schedule.** At both group sizes a tuned constant
   learning rate beat the best constant drift, with its optimum at the edge of the grid. A fixed
   learning rate produces a drift that changes over a run; that this wins says the optimal drift
   schedule is not flat.

The negative result is more informative than a win would have been: it separates the unit (drift,
which is right) from the schedule (constant, which is not) and puts a bound on the range over which
a single target can be carried.
